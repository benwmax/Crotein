The Science
of the Crow

Eight thousand years of human knowledge, distilled into one scoop.

Across cultures from the Pacific Northwest to the steppes of Central Asia, the crow has been revered not merely as a symbol of intelligence — but as a source of profound sustenance. Indigenous hunters from the Lakota to the Tlingit understood what modern sports science is only beginning to confirm: crow muscle tissue is among the most nutritionally dense, complete-protein sources available in nature. Crotein exists to carry that ancient wisdom forward.

Why Crow Protein?

The biology of an apex flier.

The American crow (Corvus brachyrhynchos) is a migratory, highly active species whose musculature is dominated by slow-twitch Type I myofibers — the same fiber type prized in endurance athletics. This muscular composition translates directly to a protein powder with an extraordinarily high proportion of oxidative, fatigue-resistant proteins that fuel sustained performance, not just short-burst hypertrophy.

Crows' migratory physiology demands exceptional iron efficiency. Their tissue naturally concentrates heme iron at rates 40–60% higher than sedentary poultry breeds — critical for oxygen transport, VO2 max, and aerobic endurance. A single serving of Crotein delivers more bioavailable iron than six servings of conventional chicken breast protein.

The crow's diet of seeds, insects, and carrion produces a uniquely broad micronutrient profile in its muscle tissue. Crotein is naturally rich in Vitamin B12 — a cofactor essential for neurological function, red blood cell formation, and DNA synthesis — as well as zinc, which supports testosterone metabolism, immune response, and protein synthesis. No synthetic vitamin spiking required. It's in the bird.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers from the flock.

What does crow protein taste like?
Crow protein has a clean, subtly gamey profile — closer to duck or dark-meat turkey than chicken breast, but milder than you might expect. In the Original Dark flavor, you'll notice a rich, slightly mineral depth. The Black Honey variant smooths this entirely with raw wildflower honey, while Smoked Salt adds a savory, umami finish that most athletes find pairs exceptionally well with water or black coffee. Overall: far more palatable than most plant proteins, and decidedly less chalky than standard whey.
Is it legal to source crow protein in the US?
A fair and frequently asked question. American crows are protected under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act — however, the Act includes explicit provisions permitting federally licensed commercial harvest under controlled conditions when conducted pursuant to a depredation order or special use permit. Crotein partners exclusively with federally licensed processors operating under MBTA Special Use Permits, renewed annually through the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service. Every harvest is documented, reported, and fully compliant. We welcome anyone to request a copy of our current permits — just contact us.
How is it different from chicken or turkey protein?
The difference is anatomical and physiological. Commercial poultry (chicken, turkey) are sedentary, selectively bred for rapid weight gain and white-meat dominance. Crows are wild, highly active, migratory birds whose musculature reflects a lifetime of demanding movement. This produces a dramatically different fiber-type distribution — more slow-twitch oxidative tissue, higher iron, superior leucine density, and a complete amino acid profile that outperforms commodity poultry protein on virtually every marker that matters to athletes.
Is it safe for people with poultry allergies?
If you have a diagnosed poultry allergy (specifically to chicken, turkey, or duck proteins), please consult your allergist before trying Crotein. Crow is an avian protein and may share certain allergenic proteins with domestic poultry — specifically alpha-parvalbumin. We do not make any claims of hypoallergenicity for individuals with documented avian protein sensitivity. Your health comes first.
How many scoops per day?
The standard recommendation is one scoop (approximately 100g dry weight) per day, providing 82g of protein. Athletes in active hypertrophy phases or those with elevated protein targets (1.6–2.2g/kg bodyweight) may consume up to two scoops daily. Because of Crotein's exceptionally high protein density per scoop, most users find a single daily serving is sufficient — and considerably more efficient than multiple scoops of conventional protein powder.
Is Crotein keto-friendly?
Yes. Original Dark contains 82g protein, 8g fat, and approximately 10g carbohydrates per 100g serving — well within ketogenic macros for most protocols. The Black Honey and Smoked Salt variants contain trace additional carbohydrates from their respective flavor additions (approximately 12g and 10.5g respectively). All three flavors are compliant with standard ketogenic frameworks (under 20g net carbs per serving).
